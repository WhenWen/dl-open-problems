# Reviewing problems

## Scope and identity

Express a problem as assumptions, setting, available inputs, target observable, intervention, and success criterion. Ask whether solving A automatically solves B, and whether the reverse holds. Bidirectional implication suggests equivalence; one direction suggests a special case; shared terminology alone proves neither.

Relations are directed from the current card: `special_case_of` points to the general problem, `depends_on` to a prerequisite within a specified approach, `related_to` to an overlapping problem, and `possibly_equivalent_to` to an unresolved identity question. `equivalent_to` requires an explanation. A norm-based approach can depend on predicting norms without asserting every possible loss predictor must use norms.

## Status

- `candidate`: a question worth investigating; literature and novelty audit incomplete.
- `literature_audited`: a dated, bounded search and full-text review support a gap; this is not proof no answer exists.
- `empirical_gap`: a public reproducible test demonstrates failure of named explanations under specified conditions, alongside a literature audit.
- `resolved`: a cited result answers the recorded question within its stated scope.
- `merged`: preserved ID redirects to another card.
- `retired`: out of scope, ill-posed, or otherwise not maintained; retain the reason.

Record promotions, resolutions, merges and exclusions in the decision log, with reviewer handles, rationale and evidence links. Self-review must be labeled. Retract or narrow an earlier conclusion when evidence changes.

## Evidence dimensions

Describe theory, empirical support and predictive validation independently. These are judgments about the precise candidate question, not all work in the area. A theorem under restrictive assumptions and strong predictive transfer are different kinds of progress. State unknowns explicitly.

## Scientific checklist

- Is the phenomenon observable, and the new setting scientifically motivated?
- What is already answered? Are theorem assumptions and practical changes explicit?
- Did the search actively look for existing solutions, synonyms and contrary evidence?
- Are explanation, retrospective fit and frozen prediction separated?
- Does the comparison control calibration budget, data access and measurement noise?
- Is there a decisive experiment or proposition, with a specified baseline and uncertainty?
- Are closest cards compared, and relationships justified?
- For an empirical gap, are configuration, seeds, evaluation protocol, predictions, raw metrics and reproduction commands public?

CI checks structure and exact duplicates only. Reviewers check semantic duplicates and scientific content. Do not interpret automated validation as completeness or novelty certification.
